# Accessibility descriptions (alt-text) for figures

For the Cambridge Accessibility Descriptions Submission Form. All figures are the author's own, generated from public data; each uses grey, black and one blue with distinct line styles so that series are distinguishable without colour.

**Figure 1.** Two line charts on logarithmic scales, 1700 to 1870, indexed to 1700 = 100, with two shaded bands marking the canal-building waves of 1760–80 and 1790–1816 and vertical lines at 1761 and 1818. Panel (a): total GDP (solid black) rises from 100 to about 1,000; population (dashed grey) rises to about 420; GDP per head (dotted blue) stays near 100 until the 1820s and reaches about 240 by 1870. Panel (b): coal output (solid black) rises to about 3,800; industrial output (dashed grey) to about 1,800; agricultural output (dotted blue) rises slowly to about 380. Both panels show output and population steepening after 1760 while GDP per head and agriculture do not.

**Figure 2.** Three panels, 1700 to 1850. Panel (a): bar chart of canal miles opened per decade, with peaks of about 350 miles in the 1770s and 550 in the 1790s and smaller bars in the 1800s and 1810s. Panel (b): cumulative canal miles, a step-like line rising from near zero to about 2,500 with steep sections in 1765–80 and 1790–1815. Panel (c): coal output per head indexed to 1700 = 100, rising to about 200 by 1790, 250 by 1800 and 550 by 1850, with a vertical line at 1833 marking when steam overtook water and wind power.

**Figure 3.** Six small line charts arranged two by three, each showing a cumulative response over horizons of 1 to 20 years with a grey 95% confidence band. Top row: canal stock to coal output, rising from about 0.3 to 0.45 with the band above zero throughout; canal stock to steam horsepower, a small positive hump peaking near 0.03 at 12 years; coal output to steam horsepower, near 0.01 with a band that includes zero after five years. Bottom row: canal stock to GDP per head, slightly negative with a band including zero; steam horsepower to GDP per head on the 1760–1830 sample, negative then rising towards zero with a wide band; canal stock to agriculture, dipping to about minus 0.08 at ten years and returning to zero.

**Figure 4.** Line chart, 1740 to 1850, of three print-frequency indices with 1850 = 100. The word canal (solid black) rises from the 1760s and is above 50 by 1810. The coal-by-water group (dashed blue) rises from the 1780s and reaches 50 around 1817. The coal-by-steam group (dotted grey) stays near zero until about 1800 and rises steeply after 1810.

**Figure 5.** Line chart, 1780 to 1830, of GDP per head indexed to 1790 = 100 for Britain (solid black), France (dashed grey) and the Netherlands (dotted blue), with the war years 1793–1815 shaded and a vertical line at 1807. Britain stays between 95 and 115 throughout. France falls to about 90 in the 1790s and recovers to about 110 by the 1820s. The Netherlands falls sharply from about 100 in 1805 to about 63 in 1808 and remains near 70 to 80 through 1830.
